Clinic No-Show Problem Analysis

Prateek Singh, Roll No. 10135 · FDE Assignment (internship-exempted)

1. Situation

Public clinics in Vitória, Brazil lose around 3,500 booked appointments a week to no-shows. Patients booked a few days ahead get an SMS reminder, but the team believes it doesn't help, because patients who got an SMS miss more often (27.6% vs 16.7%). Before dropping, keeping or extending reminders, the clinic needs to know whether they work and whether they are actually being sent. The analysis uses the clinics' real appointment records (110,527 appointments, April to June 2016), with public holiday and weather data.

5. Problem statement

Advance-booked appointments at Vitória's public clinics end in a no-show 28.5% of the time. The clinic reads its data as "SMS reminders don't work", but reminders only go to patients booked 3+ days ahead, who miss most often anyway, and on 7 days no reminders were sent at all. The clinic needs a reliable, repeatable view of whether reminders reduce no-shows and whether they are delivered, so it can decide to drop, fix or extend them.

7. MVP scope and success

AreaScope
In scopeA weekly pipeline (retrieve, validate, model, metrics), a daily alert when reminder coverage falls below 50%, and a weekly call list of patients with 2+ earlier no-shows
Out of scopeNo-show prediction or ML, overbooking rules, SMS wording, changing vendor, cost figures
SuccessIn a 4-week test against a control group (fix outages, send SMS to 1-2 day bookings, call repeat no-shows), the KPI falls by at least 2 points (to 26.5% or lower), coverage stays above 80% every day, and opt-outs do not rise

8. Why this is the right problem to solve first

I listed every problem the data showed and compared them before building anything. I did not start with a prediction model or an AI tool: the first question was which problem is worth fixing, and whether the data can support it.

Candidate problemSize (6 weeks)Can we act on it now?Decision
SMS reminders judged "not working", and not sent on 7 days60,021 eligible bookings; 24,539 got no SMSYes: the SMS already exists, the fix is a daily checkSolve first
Repeat no-show patients5% of patients, 35% of no-showsYes: a short call list for receptionPart of the MVP
Long lead times38.6% of advance bookings are 15+ days outSlowly: needs a slot policy changeLater
Rain / weather18.9% vs 20.3%, only 2 rainy daysNo: can't control it, weak evidenceNot now
No-show prediction modelno send times, no clinic or doctor fieldsNo: basics are unreliable firstOut of scope

Why reminders come first. (1) The clinic is about to decide from a misleading number: the naive comparison says SMS makes things worse, so they may stop paying for it. (2) It is cheap: the reminder is already paid for, and a daily coverage alert costs almost nothing. (3) It is big: reminding the 24,539 missed patients would have meant about 1,325 fewer no-shows, about 1.8 KPI points. (4) The other fixes depend on it: a call list or an overbooking rule can't be judged if reminders fail silently.

10. Evidence for the main conclusion

The naive view: 27.6% no-show with an SMS vs 16.7% without. But SMS only goes to bookings 3+ days ahead, and those miss more anyway. Compared at the same lead time, the picture flips. The outage days give a second check, because on those days nobody got an SMS whoever they were.
